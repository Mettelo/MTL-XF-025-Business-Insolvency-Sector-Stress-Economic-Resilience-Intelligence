# MTL-XF-025 — Business context

## Organisation and pressure

**Business Insolvency, Sector Stress & Economic Resilience Intelligence.** An economic-development team needs earlier warning of where business distress is rising by insolvency type, sector and geography so business-support resources can be investigated before pressure becomes more severe.

The simulated sponsor, Confidential Economic Development & Business Resilience Directorate, must decide where to direct limited analytical and service-review attention. Its starting problem is an inconsistent view of business insolvency, sector stress & economic resilience intelligence: totals, rates, periods and categories can lead users to different conclusions without a common definition. The engagement supplies an evidence base; it does not assume the client has already suffered a quantified failure.

## Current process and its consequences

Analysts retrieve official tables independently, prepare local summaries and discuss headline differences in management meetings. Without a shared cohort and definition register, revisions, incomparable denominators and missing coverage can pass into recommendations. The consequence is an investigation shortlist that cannot be explained or reproduced, rather than a verified estimate of financial loss.

## Required decision process

1. Confirm the reporting cohort and available dimensions.
2. Review governed metrics and quality exceptions before interpreting patterns.
3. Compare like-for-like trends and segment evidence against the questions below.
4. Challenge at least two prioritisation scenarios and document changes in shortlisted cases.
5. Assign a responsible role, evidence-gathering action and review point to every proposed investigation.

**Decision:** Which sectors or recorded geographic concentrations of company insolvency warrant business-support investigation, after distinguishing count growth, coverage and insolvency procedures?

## Business questions and evidence

| Question | Expected evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Which insolvency procedures drive recent changes within each jurisdiction? | Reconciled trend/segment table and diagnostic chart with period, grain, denominator and source references. |
| Which sectors concentrate observed records, where industry coding exists? | Reconciled trend/segment table and diagnostic chart with period, grain, denominator and source references. |
| Are recorded geographic concentrations robust to missing or ambiguous addresses? | Segment or coverage assessment stating which comparisons are supported. |
| Do recent trends persist under three- versus six-month smoothing? | Sensitivity or scope audit showing what changes the interpretation. |
| What business-population denominator and support-service evidence are needed before an early-warning claim? | Investigation plan with missing evidence, owner and decision-review trigger. |

## Intended management use

- Produce procedure/jurisdiction trends separating seasonal adjustment bases and registration dates.
- Analyse industry and geography only where the record-level fields support them; quantify unmatched and uncoded records.
- Shortlist up to ten sector/geographic investigation cases with three-/six-month sensitivity and explicit denominator gaps.

The BI executive view starts with the commissioned question, reporting period and coverage. Users inspect a diagnostic view before reading the investigation shortlist. Every case records the signal, its magnitude, uncertainty, alternative explanations, evidence still needed and a next-step owner. The final briefing must explain at least one case where the evidence is insufficient for action.

## Measures of project value

Success means a repeatable review with five supported findings, five reconciled dashboard measures, two compared decision scenarios, ten UAT scenarios and a reproducible handover. Teams may propose a future operational baseline and improvement target, but must label these as proposed measures rather than achieved benefits. No observed savings or service improvement can be claimed from this simulated engagement alone.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

- Counts do not measure insolvency risk per business without a compatible business-population denominator.
- Public company records must not become personal profiles; omit unnecessary names and addresses from outputs.
- Retrospective insolvency indicators are not validated predictive early warnings; forecasting is optional and requires time-based evaluation.

## Dependencies and risks

- Source/schema validation may narrow the scope: record an explicit impact decision rather than inventing fields.
- Data revisions may alter findings: preserve immutable source manifests and rerun comparisons.
- Small, suppressed or non-comparable segments may distort ordering: apply eligibility rules and show excluded cases.
- Local context or operational records may be missing: attach an evidence request to the relevant recommendation.
